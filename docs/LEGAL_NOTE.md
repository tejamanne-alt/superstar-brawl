# Likeness and IP: read before anything goes public

*Not legal advice. Talk to an Indian IP / media lawyer before publishing, pitching publicly or monetising.*

## The short version

Changing about half of an actor's name **does not** remove the risk if the character is meant to look "exactly like them".
What courts protect is the person's **persona**: face and likeness, voice, signature mannerisms, catch-phrases, and names
including nicknames. A stylised art style (Spider-Verse-like or otherwise) does not change that. A caricature that is
recognisable *because* it copies the person is the thing being protected.

Indian courts actively enforce this "personality right":

- **Amitabh Bachchan:** Delhi High Court interim injunction (Nov 2022) against unauthorised use of his name, image, voice
  and other attributes.
- **Rajinikanth:** Madras High Court (2015) stopped a film from using his name; his lawyers have publicly warned against
  unauthorised use of his name, image, voice and caricature.
- The same protection is open to every actor on this roster, and celebrity personality-rights cases have become frequent.

A second layer: the avatars lean on famous **film characters** (faction hero, Baasha-style don, Shahenshah-style
vigilante, masked hero, bandaged vigilante). The producers own those characters, their names and their costume designs.
This concept art uses renamed, original takes, but a released game should keep them clearly original or license them.

## Realistic paths

1. **License the stars** (endorsement or likeness deals, typically per actor). This is the only safe way to ship faces
   that look like them. It also opens marketing: their voices, launch events, social reach.
2. **Original archetypes:** keep the *genre heroes* (faction mass hero, style-icon don, angry campus rebel, supercop,
   iron-armed vigilante, veteran master, bandaged vigilante, masked speedster, dance-fighter) with **deliberately
   different faces and names**. The Indian-cinema homage then lives in moves, staging and music cues, not in identities.
3. **Hybrid:** launch with original archetypes, then add licensed "guest superstar" avatars as deals close.

## What this concept pass did

- Parody names (≈50 % match) as requested, and caricatures driven by signature traits (mustache, hair, build, wardrobe,
  props), not photographic likeness.
- No real names, logos or film titles inside the artwork. The `Inspired by` column exists only in internal docs.
- No Sony/Marvel/Spider-Man characters, logos or designs. The "glitch" motif is our own film-strip / single-screen cinema
  language.

## Art style (v2)

The v2 look borrows the *general* conventions of modern teen-comic illustration and printed-comic rendering
(halftone light, heavy ink, off-register colour). An art style as such is not protected, but specific characters,
logos and cover trade dress are. The concept art therefore uses only our own nine fighters, our own logo and
lettering, and no Archie Comics characters, names, logos or cover designs.
